Framing note: Other funders being active in an area does not mean it is covered. Additionality is assessed rigorously throughout, but the prior is that most areas are underfunded relative to burden. Methodology

Each area was researched over 20 iterations by Claude Opus 4.8 with web search, producing structured output files (mortality evidence, analogues, CEA models in Python, and synthesis). Monte Carlo models run 200,000–300,000 draws with triangular distributions anchored to the literature. Cost-effectiveness is reported as $/DALY averted (mortality-channel-only unless noted). All parameters, code, and search logs are saved in /Users/parthahya/tools/research_loop_tool/research_runs/.

Benchmarks: GiveWell top-charity bar ~$50–100/DALY; cash transfers ~$200–300/DALY; low-HDI opportunity-cost threshold ~$998/DALY (Ochalek et al.). "Cash-competitive" means <$800/DALY. Additionality-adjusted figures discount by the counterfactual fraction already covered by other funders.

What Noora Does

Noora Health's Care Companion Program (CCP) is a nurse-delivered ~40-minute group class (25–30 caregivers/session, video/flipchart-driven) at hospital discharge across India and Bangladesh. It trains family caregivers in newborn danger-sign recognition, thermal care, breastfeeding, and care-seeking. As of mid-2026: 658 facilities across all 8 Bangladesh divisions and major tertiary networks in India; 3.4M+ caregivers trained since 2020. Noora Bangladesh · Impact page.

The 7 Crux Resolutions

1. Red-teaming the 18% NMR figure

The Kumar et al. 2023 (PLOS GPH) study is an uncontrolled pre/post comparison across 28 tertiary hospitals (NMR 41.3→33.6, adj RR 0.82, CI 0.71–0.94) with a self-reported 4-week phone survey and differential non-response (72.4% pre vs 68.8% post). The de-correlated haircut ledger:

True-causal central: ~12% NMR reduction. Worst case: ~5%. GiveWell is independently funding an India pilot + neonatal-mortality RCT on Noora — i.e., the field's most rigorous evaluators judged existing evidence insufficient. An EA Forum analysis triangulates ~$1,235/life saved, noting a rigorous RCT "could move the cost per life saved several times higher."

2. Fidelity erosion under high ward load

CCP is nurse-delivered (task-added, not task-shifted). Teaching is the first discretionary task cut: Ausserhofer et al. 2014 (BMJ Qual Saf) found "educating patients and families" is left undone by 41% of nurses even in well-staffed European hospitals. India runs a ~2M nurse shortage (Sharma & Rani 2020, JFMPC).

Erosion function: f(m) = exp(−0.42·(m−1)), where m = ward-load multiplier.

3. Bangladesh — highest-additionality node

Bangladesh absorptive capacity: ~$5M/yr recurring + $2–4M one-time evaluation. Key facts: 3.0M births/yr, NMR ~20/1,000 (~60,000 neonatal deaths/yr, BDHS 2022), 65% facility delivery, 658 Noora facilities. No Bangladesh mortality endpoint exists anywhere. The Bangladesh CCP outcome study cited in Noora materials is actually from India (IDinsight). GiveWell funds India but not Bangladesh outcomes — making Bangladesh the genuinely additional node.

Important structural cap: Bangladesh NMR (20) is half India's study baseline (41.3) → less per-birth headroom → Bangladesh $/DALY is structurally worse than the India headline even under full effect transfer.

4. Bangladesh mortality proxy

No defensible Bangladesh mortality proxy exists. Best available: skin-to-skin/KMC practice uptake, which has an established mortality link (Sivanandan & Sankar 2023, BMJ GH: pooled RR 0.68; WHO iKMC, NEJM 2021: RR 0.75), but the African real-world null (OMWaNA Uganda, Lancet 2024: RR 0.84 NS) plus dose/self-report discounts leave it non-decision-grade. A funded Bangladesh eval must measure 28-day mortality via stepped-wedge with verified death ascertainment, not phone surveys.

What CHAI's Supply-Chain "AI" Actually Is

CHAI does not operate a frontier ML system. Its forecasting tools are classical statistical/spreadsheet instruments: the ARV "Simple Tool" is a morbidity-based forecasting spreadsheet, and annual global malaria commodity forecasts are a Gates-funded statistical consortium (MAP/IVCC/RBM/MMV). CHAI operates at scale — 29M people across 125 countries, 21M on HIV/TB/malaria medicines, ~$208M operating budget (CHAI 2024 Annual Report) — but the "AI" descriptor is largely peripheral. AI-centrality = 2.5/10.

A 9-model bakeoff on Ethiopian essential-medicine demand (EPSS Gondar) found XGBoost gave only "comparable model fitness with lower error estimates" vs ARIMA/classical methods (PMC13197753). The gain is a forecast-error improvement several causal links upstream of any averted death.

The Supply-Chain → Mortality Chain Is Broken

The whole supply-chain reference class stops at process metrics (availability/stockout-days/forecast accuracy), not mortality. The flagship Zambia national supply-chain cluster-RCT (Vledder et al. 2019) measured availability of tracer drugs with no mortality endpoint. Scoping reviews confirm patient outcomes are "rarely the explicit focus." A 2025 systematic review of stock-outs finds the dominant causes are funding constraints, procurement delays, weak systems, governance fragmentation, and manufacturing — with inaccurate forecasting only one of many minor contributors. Forecasting fraction driving stockouts: Tri(0.05, 0.15, 0.30).

Lenacapavir — the Single Strongest Node (No AI)

Trial-grade efficacy: PURPOSE 1 ~100% reduction (zero incident infections, cisgender women, SSA); PURPOSE 2: 96%, superior to daily Truvada. Generic $40/person-year to LMICs by 2027 via Unitaid+CHAI+Wits RHI+Dr Reddy's/Hetero vs Gilead list $28,218/PY (CHAI Sept 2025). Lancet HIV modelling (Jamieson 2024): LEN averts 12.3–18% of infections over 10 years, clearing <$500/DALY in high-incidence settings.

Honest caveats: (1) ~$100/DALY is a leverage artifact — it credits CHAI with DALYs financed by others' commodity budgets and hinges on a 2-year acceleration being real and attributable. Gilead signed direct licensing with six generic manufacturers Oct 2024, and scale-up is funded by PEPFAR + GF + CIFF + Gates ($912M). (2) Real-world PrEP service delivery ran $394–655/client-year in Zambia — the $40/PY commodity floor is not the delivered cost. Defensible figure: $100–250/DALY, additionality-gated.

TSU Fiscal-Triage — Highest-Confidence CHAI Activity

GiveWell rates CHAI Technical Support Units at 13–28× cash on a leverage basis, already funding ($4.66M, April 2025) in Burkina Faso, Malawi, Nigeria, Uganda, Zambia (GiveWell TSU grant). The post-USAID vacuum creates acute additionality: PEPFAR partner cuts >77% of implementers, GHSC-PSM terminating with no logistics successor. The TSU model — advising governments to reallocate budgets from low-CE to high-CE programs — is the value. Displacement caveat: development assistance displaces ~$0.14–0.43 of domestic health spending per dollar (Lu et al.), so 13–28× is an upper band requiring a displacement audit.

What the Commodity Rail Is

Maisha Meds operates a POS + reimbursement/subsidy engine at private drug shops, pharmacies, and clinics in Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, and Nigeria (25.6M patient visits in 2025). The RDT-conditioned dispensing program — requiring a rapid diagnostic test before ACT dispensing — moved malaria guideline adherence 8%→33% (a process metric from a reimbursement program, not an LLM effect). FP commodity distribution also moves DMPA-SC choice ~6× in a pharmacist-nudge RCT. The fundamental insight: the cheap DALYs live in getting the right commodity to the right patient via the reimbursement rail, not in any AI diagnostic layer.

Extend vs Deepen

Deepen first (~$137/DALY) < Extend (~$206/DALY) at current margins — rail cost is sunk at existing 5,000 sites; onboarding and supervision dilution raise the marginal cost of extension. But deepening saturates fast (~380 malaria cases/site/yr fixed pool). Optimal: deepen first to bridge the PMI stockout gap (~$2–3M/yr), then targeted extension into high-transmission underserved geographies.

Additionality (~0.45)

The 2025 PMI cut (~47%) and Global Fund shortfall (30% short of $18B target) raise additionality. Global Fund Uganda award to Maisha and Lagos State budget adoption partially cap it. Antivenom Quality — Key Finding

Liquid ASV is nominally stable at ≤37°C, but in real rural India (40–48°C excursions, freeze-thaw, power gaps) the cold-chain-dependence produces a ~25% field efficacy penalty vs lyophilized — driven ~3:1 by the coverage/stocking channel (~19pp) over molecular potency loss (~8pp). Portfolio action: procure lyophilized ASV for all CHC banks and ambulances. Lyophilized is ambient-stable 2–3 years and showed no significant potency loss at 56°C/1 year (PLoS NTD). This elevates Tier 2 priority and removes most of the penalty for free.

The Mechanism Debate (Load-Bearing Uncertainty)

Kremer et al. 2023 (NBER w30835) pools 15 RCTs (~25k children) → water treatment cuts all-cause U5 mortality odds ~25%. The puzzle: this exceeds what the diarrhea-specific pathway (~8–10% of U5 deaths) can explain, implying enteric-infection→immune/nutrition spillover effects — or publication/selection bias in underpowered trials (Unjournal evaluations). GiveWell uses a ~6× haircut to 4.12% (CI 1–7%) and still finds 22× cash. The whole case rides on this 1–7% band. If true effect is near the 1% floor, ILC falls below the bar even without additionality adjustment.

USAID Vacuum — Additionality Source

Pre-2025 US bilateral WASH: ~$450–500M/yr (largest globally, USAID archive). The Jan-2025 executive order dissolved USAID; WASH cut ~86% (CSIS); cholera deaths rose where contracts were cancelled (Bloomberg 2026). The counterfactually-valuable slice for a new funder: the recurring operating layer (chlorine dosing, residual monitoring, cholera response) in Francophone Africa with no domestic backfill. Not stranded infrastructure capex.

Why additionality is very high: The UK dismantled the Fleming Fund (no Phase 3 after March 2026), USAID One Health collapsed, and the PASTEUR Act ($6B pull) is stalled (4th attempt, reintroduced Feb 2026, still not passed). Critically: even if PASTEUR passes, it has no LMIC-access conditionality — a globally-accessible pull remains additional. No global pull mechanism exists.

A. Pull Mechanisms — The Biggest Prize

The antibiotic market is structurally broken: good stewardship means low volume, so a novel antibiotic's NPV is near-zero even with FDA approval. Definitive proof: Achaogen's plazomicin was FDA-approved June 2018 (projected $500M peak sales), sold <$1M in 6 months, and the company went bankrupt April 2019. Most SME antibiotic sponsors since 2010 have gone bankrupt or exited at a loss (Nature HSSC 2024).

The fix is delinkage — separating antibiotic revenue from volume sold. The Pneumococcal AMC ($1.5B) proves it works: 230–290k U5 deaths averted through 2015, >3M projected by 2030, ~$90–105/DALY on the subsidy alone. Independent modelling finds PCV at AMC prices at a global median ICER of ~$51/DALY. The UK "Netflix" subscription (£5–20M/yr/drug) proves the model is operable at national scale but is sub-therapeutic at the global level.

Right-sized pull: ~$2–4B lifetime per drug. Three independent methods converge: DRIVE-AB ($1B market-entry reward), Health Affairs 2021 ($2–4B), and Harvard Kennedy School M-RCBG WP 232 (2024) ($2.6B/10yr per drug at 11% IRR; $15.6B for 6 drugs).

LMIC-access conditionality (non-negotiable): Model on the GARDP cefiderocol licensing deal (135 countries, cost-plus generic by Orchid Pharma) — a pull without access-conditionality forfeits most of the health impact in LMICs. Any drug incentivized by the pull must have WHO-prequalification and LMIC-access terms agreed before the reward is disbursed.

Administration: GARDP (existing track record with zoliflodacin, NeoSep1), WHO, or a CEPI-analog new entity. GARDP's zoliflodacin FDA approval (Dec 2025) with licensed access for 150 mostly-LMIC countries via Innoviva demonstrates the model works end-to-end.

B. Adult Access — Anchor GARDP SECURE

The growing burden is adult gram-negative sepsis in MICs (age shift: <5 deaths −50%, ≥70 deaths +85.7%, 1990–2021). GARDP SECURE is the only dedicated antibiotic-access vehicle and is capital-starved (~€60M). Anchor it at $100–300M.

$/DALY: ~$94 on the subsidy-increment framing (philanthropy funds only the access margin; the generic drug is financed via SECURE pooled cost-plus procurement — cefiderocol already licensed generic to Orchid Pharma at cost-plus across 135 countries). Rises to ~$525/DALY on full-economic-cost. Both are cash-competitive.

C. Generic Antibiotics — Volume Guarantee

Essential off-patent antibiotics are too cheap to manufacture sustainably → shortages: pip-tazo (28.4% of shortage reports), benzathine penicillin (chronic since 2015, CMI review 2024). Critically: lack of access kills more people today than resistance — ~5.7M antibiotic-preventable deaths/yr, including ~300k treatable pneumococcal deaths in LMICs and 40.4M U5 children with treatable febrile illness where only ~70% receive antibiotics (One Health Trust 2019). A $50–150M/yr revolving volume guarantee could underwrite reliable supply of essential antibiotics. $/DALY: ~$61 (softest number — depends on stockout→death fraction).

D. Surveillance Bridge — Fleming Fund Successor

25 LMICs are donor-orphaned right now after the Fleming Fund was dismantled (Lancet Microbe 2025). National AMR surveillance system cost: ~$6.7M/yr (Nepal estimate); recurrent costs exceed 2% of government health spend in 46 LMICs (PMC12838329; Lancet ID 2024).

Bridge: $70–100M/yr via ICAN network, WHO GLASS, Africa CDC AMR program, FIND diagnostics, ReAct. The value is option/insurance — surveillance data directly changes empiric treatment guidelines which changes CFR. $/DALY: ~$306 (option value only, mortality attribution is indirect).

Endowment case: A $1.5–2.5B endowment at 5% annual return generates $75–125M/yr forever, without single-funder political risk (the exact Fleming Fund failure mode). Governance candidates: Africa CDC (regional legitimacy), WHO GLASS (global mandate), or a new purpose-built entity. At $306/DALY, the endowment is not a primary mortality bet — it is a foundational infrastructure buy that makes every other AMR intervention more effective.

E. AMR Vaccine Pipeline

Existing vaccines could avert 106k AMR-associated deaths/yr today; new TB + Klebsiella vaccines could avert +543k/yr, saving 2.5B antibiotic doses/yr (WHO 2024). The "missing middle" is Phase 2→3 for Klebsiella/Shigella/ExPEC/GBS — CARB-X does early-stage, Gavi does rollout, but no dedicated funder de-risks Phase 2→3 clinical development.

High-profile failure to absorb: J&J/Sanofi's ExPEC9V "E.mbrace" Phase 3 was discontinued Feb 2025 for insufficient efficacy ($250M Sanofi impairment). MRSA vaccines have repeatedly failed. This argues for a portfolio accelerator across 6–8 candidates, not a single-vaccine bet.

A $500M–$2B CEPI-for-AMR-vaccines accelerator would fund a diversified portfolio of Phase 2→3 candidates with LMIC-access conditions built in. $/DALY: ~$571 on bring-forward framing (conservative, because it credits only advancement of timeline, not creation of new vaccines). High-risk, high-reward; requires a portfolio approach given failure rates.

Near-term priority targets: Group B Streptococcus (GBS, maternal-neonatal; Phase 2 candidates from Pfizer, Minervax), Shigella (oral vaccine pipeline; ETEC co-benefit), Klebsiella (earliest stage but highest-mortality resistant pathogen). TCV is already Gavi-supported — do not duplicate.

Recommendations by Commitment Size

$10–50M: Regulatory harmonization (AMA/WHO-PQ) + surveillance bridge + SECURE partial. Immediate DALYs, systemic leverage, acute additionality. Best $/DALY for the capital.
$200M: The pull proof tranche (LMIC-access-conditioned) — prove the model with 1–2 drugs and trigger government co-investment. OR a blended immediate-DALY portfolio (SECURE + surveillance + generic volume guarantee). Both ~$60–94/DALY.
$500M–$1B: Pull co-investment trigger + AMR vaccine accelerator (portfolio approach) + surveillance bridge. Begin designing the standing facility governance.
$2B+: Syndicated mega-facility converting fragile aid into permanent institutions — pull facility + CEPI-for-AMR-vaccines + surveillance endowment. At this scale, philanthropy becomes the anchor that transforms the global AMR response from politically fragile to structurally durable. Leverage: 4–8× central (sovereign co-commitment model, not commercial blended finance which averages only 4.1× total).

Key Limitations

  • Pull impact uncertainty: DALYs live in access (reach × clinical benefit), not the reward size. A pull without LMIC-access conditionality forfeits most impact.
  • Vaccine failure rates: High; bring-forward CEA is conservative but multiple Phase 3 failures are likely in a portfolio.
  • Syndication leverage is precedent-inferred, not realized: The ~12× headline ceiling rests on sovereign co-commitment (CEPI/Gavi model). Empirical commercial blended finance averages only ~4.1× (Convergence). No AMR pull facility has ever been syndicated.
  • Agricultural attribution: Human-mortality chain is indirect; treat as option-value/co-benefit, not core mortality buy.
  • Neonatal gram-negative sepsis (prior run): The $388/DALY neonatal access bundle (GARDP NeoSep1 + SECURE) from the first AMR run remains valid as a standalone $40–60M entry point pending NeoSep1 readout (~2028).
