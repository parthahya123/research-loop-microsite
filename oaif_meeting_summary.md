## Proposals by Organization

### 1. Noora Health
**Grant ask: ~$50M**

**What they do:** Noora Health teaches family caregivers of hospitalized patients in India and Bangladesh how to manage recovery and respond to health emergencies at home — targeting the moment when families are most receptive to learning. They focus on newborn care, postpartum hemorrhage response, and maternal nutrition, reaching families in government hospital maternity wards. They have reached over 11 million caregivers across 400+ hospitals in India and Bangladesh.

**What the proposal says:** Noora proposes to scale their AI-powered "Noora Care" chatbot, which provides personalized follow-up coaching to caregivers after discharge via WhatsApp, extending the in-hospital learning through the critical first weeks at home. The grant would fund deployment to an additional 5 million caregivers, AI model development and localization into new languages, and an RCT to generate causal mortality evidence for the AI-enhanced model. A stepped-wedge cluster RCT is currently underway in Karnataka; results expected in 2026.

---

### 2. PATH — AI HIV Health Worker Training (STRIPE)
**Grant ask: ~$17.5M over 4 years**

**What they do:** PATH is a global health nonprofit working across vaccines, diagnostics, digital health, and health systems. Their STRIPE program is a validated HIV training curriculum with 27 modules (English/French/Portuguese) developed for health workers across Sub-Saharan Africa. STRIPE has reached 17,759 learners through facilitated in-person delivery; an observational study found in-person delivery produces ~2× the knowledge gain of virtual/self-paced formats.

**What the proposal says:** PATH proposes to build an AI-enabled Learning Management System that uses AI simulation and interactive roleplay to close the interactivity gap between in-person and virtual training — targeting >250,000 health workers across 12 SSA countries in HIV care. The Rwanda Year 1 sprint would deploy to ~80,000 CHWs and clinicians through the Rwanda Biomedical Centre. The ask covers platform development, AI simulation design, deployment infrastructure, and a stepped-wedge RCT comparing AI simulation vs. in-person training. The stated goal is to reduce per-worker training cost from $833–2,489 to <$120.

---

### 3. Nexleaf Analytics
**Grant ask: ~$5M**

**What they do:** Nexleaf builds IoT sensor systems for global health supply chains, primarily ColdTrace — a cold-chain temperature monitoring system that continuously logs vaccine refrigerator temperatures in LMIC health facilities and alerts facility staff and district managers to failures in real time. They operate in 20+ countries, with >10,000 devices deployed in government facilities.

**What the proposal says:** The grant would fund expansion of ColdTrace into high-burden countries with documented cold-chain failures, integration of AI-predictive maintenance alerts (flagging fridges likely to fail before they do), and dashboard tools for national cold-chain program managers. The evidence base for cold-chain monitoring improving effective vaccine coverage is well-established; the proposal targets the next layer — moving from monitoring to prediction and automated procurement triggers.

---

### 4. Rocket Learning
**Grant ask: ~$12M**

**What they do:** Rocket Learning sends daily WhatsApp play-and-learn activities to parents of children aged 0–6 through India's government Anganwadi early childhood system, supporting parents in delivering stimulation activities at home. They reach ~12 million households through integration with the government's digital platform and have evidence of improved Early Childhood Development outcomes across numeracy, literacy, and social-emotional indicators.

**What the proposal says:** The proposal seeks funding to expand AI-personalization of content delivery — adapting messages to each child's developmental stage and each parent's engagement patterns — and to integrate with the government's NIPUN Bharat foundational literacy-numeracy mission. The AI layer would use engagement analytics to optimize which activities drive the strongest learning outcomes and to identify children at developmental risk for targeted follow-up by Anganwadi workers.

---

### 5. Intelehealth
**Grant ask: ~$10M**

**What they do:** Intelehealth is an India/Nepal-based nonprofit building AI-assisted telemedicine infrastructure that connects rural patients (via ASHA community health workers) to qualified doctors through a structured clinical decision support system. Their platform uses AI triage to guide CHWs through symptom collection and generates preliminary assessments that doctors review remotely. They operate at scale in Madhya Pradesh, UP, Rajasthan, and Nepal.

**What the proposal says:** The proposal funds deployment of AI triage to an additional 15,000 CHWs, development of AI clinical decision support for specific high-burden conditions (pneumonia, diarrhea, TB screening, maternal danger signs), and a clinical validation study comparing AI-assisted CHW consultations versus standard telemedicine. A key ambition is demonstrating that AI triage can safely expand the scope of CHW clinical practice beyond current protocol-defined limits.

---

### 6. CHAI — AI Health Systems Strengthening
**Grant ask: ~$15M**

**What they do:** The Clinton Health Access Initiative works with governments across Africa and Asia to strengthen health systems through technical assistance, supply chain optimization, and health data analytics. CHAI is embedded in ministries of health across 36+ countries and has a track record of improving commodity procurement, program management, and disease surveillance.

**What the proposal says:** CHAI proposes to build an AI-powered health analytics platform embedded directly in government ministries — surfacing insights from routine health data (DHIS2, facility EMRs, supply chain systems) to identify program performance gaps, predict stockouts, and guide resource allocation. The proposal covers three countries (Malawi, Ethiopia, Sierra Leone) over 3 years, with a model of AI tools being owned and operated by government data teams rather than by CHAI.

### 7. Maisha Meds
**Grant ask: ~$20M over 24 months**

**What they do:** Maisha Meds runs a financial-incentive program for private pharmacies across Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, and Nigeria that reimburses pharmacists for conducting malaria rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs) before dispensing antimalarials. Their AI computer-vision system reads RDT photos to verify results and enable fraud-resistant reimbursement at scale. A published NBER RCT (Dieci et al., 2026) found testing rates tripled (8%→33%) and inappropriate ACT dispensing to test-negatives fell significantly across 140 Kenyan pharmacies.

**What the proposal says:** The grant would expand the program from 5,700 to 15,000+ pharmacy sites across Nigeria and Uganda (both high-malaria-burden, high-private-sector-share markets), fund development of an LLM-based clinical decision support tool for pharmacy staff (gated on RCT evidence), and generate health outcome evidence for a future government co-investment case. The proposal also covers integration with national malaria program reporting systems (DHIS2, HMIS).

### 8. M-Mama (Pathfinder International)
**Grant ask: ~$14M**

**What they do:** M-Mama operates an emergency maternal transport system in Tanzania and Malawi that connects women experiencing obstetric emergencies to ride-hailing drivers via an AI-enabled dispatch system. Community health workers and facility staff can trigger emergency transport through a simple call or SMS; AI route optimization dispatches the nearest available vehicle. They have facilitated over 50,000 emergency transports.

**What the proposal says:** The grant funds expansion to three new regions in Tanzania, development of an AI "early warning" system that flags women at high risk of obstetric emergency based on antenatal care data before emergencies occur, and a formal health outcome evaluation. The proposal emphasizes the PEPFAR funding gap creating an opportunity window for private maternal care investment.

### 9. Causal Foundry
**Grant ask: ~$8M**

**What they do:** Causal Foundry builds AI health intelligence infrastructure — connecting facility EMRs, community health data, and supply chain systems into unified analytics platforms for government health programs in Africa and Asia. Their core product surfaces predictive risk scores (sepsis, maternal mortality, medication non-adherence) at the point of care and flags patients for proactive outreach. They work with governments in Kenya, Ghana, Rwanda, and India.

**What the proposal says:** The proposal covers two programs: (1) Kenya CHW program — AI sepsis and high-risk pregnancy prediction for community health workers, targeting 10,000 CHWs in Kisumu and Mombasa counties, and (2) Rwanda government capitation analytics — AI-powered targeting of preventive care visits under the community-based health insurance program. The grant funds data engineering, model development, clinician training, and a prospective cohort study.

### 10. Jacaranda Health — PROMPTS
**Grant ask: ~$12M**

**What they do:** Jacaranda Health runs a network of maternal health clinics in Kenya and also develops and scales digital health tools for the broader Kenyan public system. Their PROMPTS program delivers structured SMS messages to pregnant women with appointment reminders, danger-sign education, and facility-birth encouragement, operating through a partnership with the Kenyan Ministry of Health at national scale.

**What the proposal says:** The proposal adds an AI chatbot layer to PROMPTS — allowing women to ask questions, receive personalized guidance, and connect to human support workers when needed — and funds expansion to rural Kenya where facility birth rates are lowest. The AI upgrade is designed specifically to address engagement dropout in the final trimester, identified as the highest-risk window for home delivery. The proposal includes integration with the government's Linda Mama free maternity care program.

---

### 11. Pinky Promise
**Grant ask: ~$5M**

**What they do:** Pinky Promise is an AI gynecology platform in India providing women with access to teleconsultation, AI-powered menstrual health tracking, fertility guidance, and sexual and reproductive health information via a mobile app. They serve predominantly urban and peri-urban Indian women who lack access to ob-gyn specialists, offering both AI self-assessment tools and connections to qualified doctors.

**What the proposal says:** The grant would fund expansion into lower-income geographies (Tier 2/3 cities and semi-urban areas), development of vernacular language AI consultation tools in 8 Indian languages, and integration with government ASHA worker referral pathways for reproductive health. The proposal positions the grant as enabling a transition from a paid-user commercial model to a subsidized public-access model for women below a certain income threshold.

---

### 12. SGHI EMPOWER — Cancer Navigation
**Grant ask: ~$10M**

**What they do:** Strathmore Global Health Institute (SGHI) in Kenya operates EMPOWER, an AI-assisted cancer patient navigation program that helps patients move through the diagnosis, staging, and treatment pathway at Kenyan public cancer centers. Over 80% of Kenyan cancer patients are diagnosed at Stage III or IV; EMPOWER uses AI to triage patients, coordinate appointments across fragmented care systems, and support adherence to chemotherapy protocols.

**What the proposal says:** The grant funds expansion of EMPOWER from 3 to 12 Kenyan county cancer centers, development of an AI early-detection screening tool for high-burden cancers (cervical, breast), and a health economics evaluation of navigation impact on treatment completion rates and survival. SGHI proposes to use the grant to establish a regional model for AI-assisted cancer care navigation that can be replicated across East Africa.

### 13. Reach Digital Health — ReachMAMA
**Grant ask: ~$8M over 24 months**

**What they do:** Reach Digital Health builds voice-first AI health platforms for low-literacy populations in Sub-Saharan Africa. Their ReachMAMA platform uses speech recognition, RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), and text-to-speech to deliver maternal health information in local languages to women without smartphones or reading ability. They operate in South Africa, Mozambique, and Nigeria.

**What the proposal says:** The proposal targets 7.75 million users across three countries over 2 years, at ~$0.52/active user/year. The AI system allows women to ask free-form questions about their pregnancy and receive contextually appropriate answers in their language, rather than receiving one-way scripted messages. The grant covers AI model development, voice dataset collection in local languages, MNO (mobile network operator) partnership costs, and an embedded evaluation.

### 14. PATH — AI-POCUS Breast Cancer Detection
**Grant ask: ~$20M**

**What they do:** PATH's diagnostics group develops and validates point-of-care diagnostic tools for LMIC settings. Their AI-POCUS (AI-assisted point-of-care ultrasound) program trains community health workers to perform basic ultrasound examinations with AI interpretation — eliminating the need for a radiologist — to detect breast masses and other conditions at primary care level.

**What the proposal says:** The proposal funds a 5-country Africa deployment of AI-POCUS for breast cancer screening, covering CHW training, device procurement (portable ultrasound probes), AI interpretation software licensing, quality assurance infrastructure, and linkage to treatment for screen-positives. The stated goal is to move breast cancer screening from a hospital-specialist function to a community-level function, addressing the 80%+ late-stage diagnosis rate in SSA.

### 15. Audere — AI HIV/SRH Companion
**Grant ask: ~$19.6M over 3 years**

**What they do:** Audere is a Seattle-based AI/software nonprofit building digital health products for HIV prevention and treatment in Sub-Saharan Africa. Their products include Aimee (a WhatsApp AI companion for AGYW on HIV prevention and PrEP uptake in South Africa), Coach Mpilo (an ART adherence coach), and an AI-assisted HIV self-test reading tool. Audere was founded by former Microsoft/Facebook engineers and is Gates-funded.

**What the proposal says:** The grant would scale the Aimee and Coach Mpilo platforms from ~160,000 to 3,000,000 users across South Africa, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Eswatini, Kenya, and Uganda. The $2/user cost model is designed for sustainability through government co-investment. The proposal covers AI model development, partnership with national DoH programs, and an outcomes evaluation study. The proposal highlights the PEPFAR funding gap as creating urgent demand for low-cost AI-enabled HIV support tools.

### 16. Penda Health — AI Consult
**Grant ask: ~$22M over 36 months**

**What they do:** Penda Health is a for-profit social enterprise running 20 primary care clinics in Nairobi, Kenya, delivering ~1,500 patient visits/day (~3M cumulative). Their AI Consult system is a GPT-4o-based clinical decision support tool embedded in their EMR that flags potential diagnostic and treatment errors in real time for reviewing clinicians. A published study in Nature Medicine (cluster QI design, 15 clinics, 106 clinicians, 39,849 visits) found 16% fewer diagnostic errors and 13% fewer treatment errors compared to controls.

**What the proposal says:** The grant funds expansion of Penda's clinic network from 20 to 50 clinics (bringing AI Consult to 2,500+ additional daily visits), deployment of AI Consult to 3 non-Penda clinic networks as a public good, and an independent health-outcome trial. The proposal argues that the $0.04/visit AI tool needs philanthropic support to spread beyond Penda's commercially-financed network into public-sector and lower-income private providers.

### 17. Zipline — Nigeria Blood & Medicine Delivery
**Grant ask: ~$50M**

**What they do:** Zipline operates autonomous drone delivery networks for blood products, vaccines, and essential medicines in Rwanda, Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Côte d'Ivoire, and the United States. Their system delivers to hospitals and health facilities that lack reliable road access or cold-chain storage, enabling on-demand delivery of blood products for emergency transfusions and vaccines for immunization programs. Their Rwanda operations cover all national blood product delivery.

**What the proposal says:** The grant funds expansion of Zipline's Nigeria operations from 2 distribution centers to 8, covering the high-burden Northern Nigeria states with the weakest road and cold-chain infrastructure. The proposal includes AI route optimization and predictive demand analytics to pre-position blood products in distribution centers ahead of demand spikes. The philanthropic funding is structured to crowd in government and insurance co-payment revenue at scale.

### 18. ImpulsoGov — Brazil AI Health Analytics
**Grant ask: ~$20M over 3 years**

**What they do:** ImpulsoGov is a Brazilian nonprofit building AI-powered data analytics for Brazil's Unified Health System (SUS), the world's largest public health system. Their tools improve targeting of preventive health programs — cervical cancer screening, vaccination catch-up, and maternal care — by analyzing individual-level data to identify who has missed services and guide community health worker outreach.

**What the proposal says:** The proposal funds national deployment of ImpulsoGov's cervical cancer AI targeting tool, expansion into 4 additional health programs (vaccination, maternal care, hypertension, diabetes), and embedding of AI analytics teams inside Ministry of Health state secretariats. A published RCT found the cervical cancer tool increased screening coverage 4-fold in intervention municipalities. The grant is designed to establish ImpulsoGov as the government's permanent AI analytics partner rather than a one-off project.

### 19. ARMMAN — mMitra AI Upgrade
**Grant ask: ~$5M**

**What they do:** ARMMAN is an Indian nonprofit whose mMitra program delivers stage-based voice IVR messages to pregnant and postpartum women through a network of government-partnered health programs, reaching millions of women across 21 Indian states. Their SAHELI AI system (developed with Google DeepMind/Harvard) uses a Restless Multi-Armed Bandit algorithm to allocate scarce live-call-worker slots to women most at risk of dropping out of the program, with RCT evidence showing 32% reduction in engagement dropout.

**What the proposal says:** The grant would fund three AI upgrades: (1) content personalization — tailoring message content to each woman's individual context, (2) WhatsApp multimedia expansion — adding richer content beyond voice IVR, and (3) scale deployment of SAHELI to the full mMitra user base. The proposal also includes a "Beyond Listenership" evaluation to test whether engagement gains translate to health behavior changes. ARMMAN currently reaches ~2.3M women directly and has helped build Kilkari, the government IVR program reaching 60M+.

### 20. The Agency Fund — Turn.io / 100 Million Mothers Coalition
**Grant ask: ~$50M over 3 years**

**What they do:** The Agency Fund is a philanthropic intermediary that incubates and accelerates digital health organizations. Their "100 Million Mothers" initiative brings together 6 leading mHealth nonprofits — Noora Health (India, caregiver education), ImpulsoGov (Brazil, health analytics), Jacaranda Health (Kenya, maternal messaging), Reach Digital Health (SSA, voice AI), Rocket Learning (India, ECD), and Parenting for Lifelong Health (global, parenting support) — to coordinate shared infrastructure, evaluation, and distribution.

**What the proposal says:** The grant funds a shared AI platform (Turn.io, a WhatsApp Business API platform), shared evaluation frameworks, MNO partnership costs to reach 15M new users across 35 countries, and coordination infrastructure. The Agency Fund's own analysis projects the coalition will reach 100M mothers by 2030. The proposal frames coordination as multiplicative — enabling constituent organizations to reach populations they couldn't access independently and to share AI models and evidence.

### 21. PHIT Tanzania — Presidential Health Innovation and Transformation
**Grant ask: ~$25M**

**What they do:** PHIT is a Tanzanian government-aligned health innovation initiative that embeds AI clinical decision support tools in primary health facilities across Tanzania. Their focus is on reducing diagnostic errors and improving clinical guideline adherence in facilities with limited specialist oversight, using AI tools embedded in the national facility EMR (GoT-HOMeR).

**What the proposal says:** The grant funds national rollout of AI clinical decision support across Tanzania's primary health network (~7,000 facilities), covering AI software licensing, EMR integration, health worker training, and infrastructure upgrades for connectivity. The proposal builds on a preliminary deployment in 20 pilot facilities and is structured as a public-sector AI adoption program with a pathway to government ownership and budget absorption after 3 years.

### 22. Orbit Health — CONNECT Ethiopia
**Grant ask: ~$18M over 24 months**

**What they do:** Orbit Health builds household-to-facility coordination systems for community health programs in Sub-Saharan Africa. Their CONNECT platform integrates community health data (eCHIS), facility EMRs, and aggregate health management systems (DHIS2) with AI risk stratification tools that help Health Extension Workers (HEWs) prioritize household visits and track referral outcomes.

**What the proposal says:** The grant funds deployment of CONNECT to 500,000 households (2.5M people) across 25 woredas in Oromia, Ethiopia, at ~$36/household over 2 years. Key features include AI family-risk ranking, closed-loop referral tracking, multilingual voice tools in Afaan Oromo, and anomaly detection for disease surveillance. The proposal explicitly requests philanthropic funding to generate evidence that will inform government and Global Fund investment in scaled deployment.

### 23. SaveLife Foundation — AI Road Safety India
**Grant ask: ~$10M over 5 years**

**What they do:** SaveLife Foundation (SLF) is India's leading road safety nonprofit, working since 2008 to reduce road traffic deaths through policy reform, evidence-based implementation, and government capacity-building. India loses 183,000 people annually to road crashes — the world's highest national toll. SLF's achievements include India's Good Samaritan Law (2016), constitutional Right to Trauma Care (2026), and the Zero-Fatality Corridor model which achieved a 55% reduction in deaths on the Mumbai-Pune Expressway over nine years. In 2026, India's Ministry of Road Transport adopted SLF's Zero Fatality District model as the national framework for the country's 100 highest-fatality districts. Their AI platform Margdarshak ingests the national crash database (~800 districts) and provides district administrators, police, engineers, and EMS coordinators with a shared action tracking system.

**What the proposal says:** The $10M grant (part of a larger $40M Zero Fatality District programme, with $15M already committed) funds the AI and technology layer needed to scale expert road-safety capability to all 100 highest-burden districts. SLF seeks to build out Margdarshak with predictive crash-risk models, a police crash-investigation assistant (Surakshak), a trauma care trainer (Rakshak), an EMS District Planner, and multilingual voice tools for field data collection. The investment supports both technology development and government adoption infrastructure — Technical Support Units, regional-language training, and state academy capacity. Year 1–2 focus: validate capabilities across 10 early-adopter districts. Year 3–5 focus: national deployment across 100 highest-burden districts with government ownership and sustained operation. SLF's demonstrated reach: 91.8 million road users at $0.04/person, mobilizing $10 of government road-safety spending per philanthropic dollar.
