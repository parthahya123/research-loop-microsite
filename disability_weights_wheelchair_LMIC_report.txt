================================================================================
DISABILITY WEIGHTS FOR WHEELCHAIR USERS IN LMICs
Deep Research Investigation — August 2026
================================================================================

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
No direct, LMIC-specific disability weight for wheelchair users exists anywhere
in the published literature. This is not a gap at the margin — it is a void at
the center of the cost-effectiveness case for wheelchair provision. The entire
evidence chain rests on proxy values derived from UK powered-wheelchair QALY
data and GBD health states that do not map cleanly to "mobility-impaired person
receives a manual wheelchair in an LMIC." A purpose-built study would cost
~$150K-$350K, take 12-18 months, and could reduce the $/DALY uncertainty band
from ~7x to ~2x. The value of information is extremely high relative to cost.

================================================================================
1. EXISTING GBD DISABILITY WEIGHTS FOR MOBILITY IMPAIRMENT
================================================================================

The GBD uses a fixed set of ~230 health states with disability weights (DWs)
derived from lay population surveys. DWs are on a 0-1 scale (0 = full health,
1 = death). The key values for mobility-related conditions:

SPINAL CORD INJURY (GBD 2013/2019):
  - Treated, below neck level:    0.296  (UI: 0.198-0.414)
  - Treated, at neck level:       0.589  (UI: 0.415-0.748)
  - Untreated, below neck level:  0.623  (UI: 0.434-0.785)
  - Untreated, at neck level:     0.732  (UI: 0.544-0.871)

MOTOR IMPAIRMENT (generic, GBD 2013):
  - Mild:      0.010  (UI: 0.006-0.016)
  - Moderate:  0.061  (UI: 0.040-0.089)
  - Severe:    0.402  (UI: 0.268-0.545)

MUSCULOSKELETAL, LEGS (GBD 2013/2019):
  - Moderate:  0.079  (UI: 0.054-0.110)
  - Severe:    0.165  (UI: 0.112-0.232)

AMPUTATION (GBD):
  - Long-term, lower limb (traumatic):  ~0.300

KEY INSIGHT: The commonly cited ~0.406 baseline for paraplegia/severe mobility
impairment is consistent with GBD "severe motor impairment" (0.402) or midway
between "treated SCI below neck" (0.296) and "untreated SCI below neck" (0.623).
Neither state precisely describes "LMIC person with mobility impairment who
could benefit from a wheelchair but doesn't have one."

CRITICAL GAP: GBD health states describe symptoms and functional limitations
via lay vignettes — they do NOT describe "person with X condition who receives
Y intervention." There is no GBD health state for "wheelchair user" nor for
"person who needs a wheelchair but lacks one." The DW reduction from wheelchair
provision must be INFERRED, not measured.

The lay descriptions used in GBD paired comparisons are brief, non-clinical
statements like: "has major difficulty walking, standing, lifting and carrying
heavy things." They do not specify whether the person has or lacks assistive
technology.

2. QALY/UTILITY STUDIES FOR WHEELCHAIR USERS IN LMICs
================================================================================

FINDING: There are NO preference-based utility studies (EQ-5D, SF-6D, HUI)
specifically measuring QALYs for manual wheelchair users in LMIC settings.

The closest evidence:

a) NIGERIA (2016): Olaleye et al. measured SF-36 in 41 SCI patients vs. 40
   controls. SCI patients had significantly lower HRQoL across all domains.
   No utility/QALY conversion reported. 51.3% were "more or less satisfied"
   with their wheelchair; only 2.6% "very satisfied."

b) JAPAN (2019): EQ-5D-5L in SCI patients showed utility improvement from
   acute to discharge: 0.325 → 0.580 for mid/lower thoracic SCI.
   (High-income country, not LMIC.)

c) THAILAND: Wheelchair users with air-filled cushions had mean utility 4x
   higher than foam cushion users (0.131 vs 0.032 on EQ-5D).
   (Middle-income country but small sample, specific to cushion type.)

d) ETHIOPIA: EQ-5D studies exist in the country but none specific to
   wheelchair users or mobility device recipients.

e) UGANDA (2024): Cross-sectional QoL study of traumatic SCI patients exists
   but did not use preference-based utility instruments amenable to QALY
   calculation.

f) UK POWERED WHEELCHAIR DATA: The ~0.27 QALYs/year gain used in the current
   CEA comes from UK studies of powered (not manual) wheelchairs, in a
   high-income context with paved infrastructure, accessible buildings, and
   support services. Adjusting this to ~0.19 for LMIC manual wheelchairs is
   a reasonable direction but entirely judgment-based.

BOTTOM LINE: The QALY gain from wheelchair provision in LMICs is genuinely
unmeasured. Every number in use is a proxy or expert assumption.

3. GBD DISABILITY WEIGHT METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

HOW DWs ARE DERIVED:
The GBD disability weights come from a specific measurement approach developed
by Salomon, Vos, Haagsma et al. (Lancet, 2012; Lancet Global Health, 2015):

Step 1: HEALTH STATE DESCRIPTIONS
  - Brief lay descriptions (2-3 sentences) of each health state
  - Non-clinical language focused on functional consequences
  - Example for severe motor impairment: describes difficulty walking,
    standing, lifting, carrying heavy things

Step 2: PAIRED COMPARISON SURVEYS
  - Respondents see two health state vignettes side by side
  - Asked: "Which person do you consider healthier overall?"
  - Binary choice — simple for lay respondents with any literacy level

Step 3: POPULATION HEALTH EQUIVALENCE (PHE) QUESTIONS
  - Used to anchor the scale (web-based surveys only)
  - Asks respondents to compare health benefits of two hypothetical programs
  - Used to map paired comparison results onto 0-1 scale

Step 4: STATISTICAL ANALYSIS
  - Probit regression on paired comparison data
  - Cardinal values estimated from ordinal rankings
  - PHE data provides scale anchoring

SURVEY IMPLEMENTATION FOR GBD 2010:
  - Household surveys in 5 countries: Bangladesh, Indonesia, Peru, Tanzania, USA
  - Face-to-face interviews (LMIC countries), telephone (USA)
  - Conducted Oct 2009 - June 2010
  - Total: 13,902 household survey respondents + 16,328 web respondents
  - 108 health states evaluated
  - Correlations between individual surveys and pooled data: r ≥ 0.9
    (except Bangladesh at r = 0.75)

FOR GBD 2013 UPDATE:
  - Added web-based surveys in 4 European countries
    (Hungary, Italy, Netherlands, Sweden)
  - 30,660 respondents in European survey alone
  - Updated to 235 health states with revised lay descriptions

KEY FINDING ON CROSS-CULTURAL VALIDITY:
The GBD 2010 study found that "comparative assessments of different disabling
sequelae, as revealed in paired comparisons, are similar in samples that vary
with respect to cultural, educational, environmental, and demographic
circumstances." This is important — it suggests a smaller LMIC-specific study
could produce results broadly consistent with GBD values, but would capture
any context-specific divergence.

However, critics note (Voigt & King, BMC 2014) that some LMIC household
survey results DID diverge meaningfully (Bangladesh r=0.75), and that the
claim of "common values" may overstate cross-cultural agreement.

4. PUBLISHED ECONOMIC EVALUATIONS OF WHEELCHAIR PROVISION
================================================================================

SYSTEMATIC REVIEW FINDING (Boggs et al., 2021):
"Only two full health economic analyses of wheelchair interventions have been
conducted and both focused on powered wheelchair provision."

This is the most damning finding. In the entire global literature, there are
only 2 full CEAs for wheelchairs, both for powered chairs in high-income
settings. There is NO published cost-per-DALY or cost-per-QALY analysis for
manual wheelchair provision in any LMIC.

Identified studies and related evidence:

a) SHORE (2017): "The long-term impact of wheelchair delivery on the lives
   of people with disabilities in three countries" — measured impacts but
   not a formal CEA with $/DALY.

b) INDONESIA STUDIES:
   - Pratiwi et al. (2019): "The economic impacts of wheelchair use: Evidence
     from Central Java, Indonesia" — measured economic gains of wheelchair
     users via cross-sectional survey. Not a formal CEA.
   - Borg et al. (2020): "Exploratory investigation of the outcomes of
     wheelchair provision through two service models in Indonesia" — compared
     WHO 8-step model vs. waiting list. Women receiving wheelchairs via WHO
     approach had better QoL. No formal CEA.

c) TAJIKISTAN (2020): Borg, Khasnabis et al. "Sustainable and equitable
   provision of wheelchairs in low- and middle-income countries: an economic
   assessment of the models for wheelchair provision in Tajikistan."
   - Compared wheelchair importation vs. local production models
   - Focused on supply-chain economics, NOT health outcome CEA
   - Published under AT2030 programme

d) UK MOTABILITY/FRONTIER ECONOMICS (2023): "The Value of a Wheelchair"
   - Social Return on Investment (SROI) approach
   - Found annual benefits of £15,200 per user per year
   - UK context, powered + manual chairs, not $/DALY framework

e) COMMUNITY-BASED DISABILITY REHABILITATION (various):
   - One study found $224/DALY averted for a comprehensive disability
     rehabilitation programme (includes but is not limited to wheelchairs)
   - Not wheelchair-specific

THERE IS NO PUBLISHED $/DALY FOR MANUAL WHEELCHAIR PROVISION IN AN LMIC.

5. WHAT WOULD IT TAKE TO GENERATE A PROPER DISABILITY WEIGHT STUDY?
================================================================================

OPTION A: GBD-COMPATIBLE PAIRED COMPARISON STUDY (GOLD STANDARD)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Design: Household paired comparison survey in 2-3 LMIC countries

Methodology:
  - Write lay vignettes for wheelchair-relevant health states:
    * "Severe mobility impairment, no wheelchair"
    * "Severe mobility impairment, with appropriate manual wheelchair"
    * "Moderate mobility impairment, no wheelchair"
    * "Moderate mobility impairment, with appropriate manual wheelchair"
  - Include ~20-30 GBD reference health states for anchoring
  - Paired comparison format identical to GBD methodology
  - Face-to-face household interviews in LMIC communities

Sample size:
  - GBD used ~2,500-3,000 per country for household surveys
  - European DW studies used ~7,500-8,000 per country (web-based)
  - Minimum viable: ~500-1,000 per country, 2 countries = 1,000-2,000 total
  - This is adequate because you're estimating DWs for 4-6 specific states,
    not the full 230+ state GBD set

Estimated cost:
  - Survey design + vignette development: $30K-$50K
  - Fieldwork (2 countries, ~1,000 respondents each): $50K-$100K
    ($25-$50 per completed household interview in LMICs)
  - Analysis + writing: $30K-$50K
  - PI time + overhead: $40K-$80K
  - Total: $150K-$280K
  - Timeline: 12-18 months

Precedent: The India study (Mathur et al., 2022, Frontiers in Public Health)
conducted a disability weights measurement study with 2,055 community members
in two Indian states (Odisha and Telangana) for 14 health states. This was
done at modest cost using visual analog scale and card sort methods.

OPTION B: EQ-5D UTILITY STUDY (FASTER, MORE DIRECTLY USEFUL FOR CEA)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Design: Before-after or cross-sectional study measuring EQ-5D in wheelchair
recipients in 2-3 LMIC countries

Methodology:
  - Administer EQ-5D-5L to:
    * People awaiting wheelchair provision (baseline)
    * People who received wheelchairs 3-12 months ago (comparison)
  - Alternatively: prospective cohort, EQ-5D at baseline + 3mo + 12mo
  - Country-specific EQ-5D value sets now exist for: Ethiopia, Kenya,
    Indonesia, India, Nigeria, Bangladesh, and others

Sample size:
  - For detecting a 0.1 utility difference with 80% power: ~130-170 per group
  - Across 2-3 countries: 400-1,000 total participants

Estimated cost:
  - Study design + ethical approval: $20K-$30K
  - Fieldwork: $40K-$80K (piggybacked on existing wheelchair programs)
  - Analysis: $20K-$30K
  - Total: $80K-$150K
  - Timeline: 12-18 months (6 months if cross-sectional only)

This directly produces a QALY gain estimate usable in CEAs.

OPTION C: HYBRID (BOTH OPTIONS COMBINED)
-----------------------------------------
Run an EQ-5D study within wheelchair programs AND a paired comparison
community survey with wheelchair-specific vignettes.
  - Total cost: $200K-$350K
  - Produces BOTH GBD-compatible DW AND CEA-ready QALY estimates
  - Maximum credibility and usefulness
  - This is the recommended approach.

WHO HAS DONE SIMILAR STUDIES?
  - Salomon et al. (GBD DW team) — gold standard methodology, Harvard/IHME
  - Haagsma et al. (European DW study) — 30,660 respondents, Erasmus MC
  - Mathur et al. (India DW study 2022) — modest-scale LMIC implementation
  - Kim et al. (South Korea DW study) — 2,728 household + 3,188 web
  - Neethling et al. (South Africa, 2016) — DW household survey in low
    socioeconomic setting, compared to GBD 2010 values

7. THE "ANALOGUE INTERVENTION" QUESTION
================================================================================

How does disability weight evidence compare for other assistive technologies?

SPECTACLES/EYEGLASSES:
  - GBD has well-established DWs for vision impairment by severity:
    * Mild: 0.004, Moderate: 0.031, Severe: 0.184, Blindness: 0.187
  - Published cost per DALY averted:
    * Reading glasses/presbyopia: ~$197/DALY averted
    * Eye camps in India: $143/DALY averted (ICER)
    * Vision centers: $262/DALY averted
  - KEY LESSON: Vision correction has the most mature CEA evidence base
    among assistive technologies, partly because DWs for "uncorrected" and
    "corrected" vision are well-established in GBD.

HEARING AIDS:
  - GBD DWs for hearing loss exist by severity level
  - Published cost per DALY: ~$720-$900 (India study)
  - BUT: "Evidence from LMICs remains limited, and uncertainty persists
    regarding locally valid utility estimates" (Lancet eClinMedicine, 2021)
  - KEY LESSON: Even hearing aids — much more studied than wheelchairs —
    face the same LMIC utility evidence gap.

PROSTHETICS (LOWER LIMB):
  - GBD DWs for amputation: ~0.300 (long-term, lower limb)
  - "No studies on prosthetic cost-effectiveness have been conducted in
    LMICs, despite prosthetic needs being greatest in resource-austere
    settings" (Cost Analyses of Prosthetic Devices, 2021)
  - KEY LESSON: The evidence desert is even worse for prosthetics than
    for wheelchairs.

OVERALL PATTERN:
  - Spectacles: Good DW evidence → good CEAs → clear case for provision
  - Hearing aids: Moderate DW evidence → some CEAs → evidence gaps in LMICs
  - Wheelchairs: Poor DW evidence → almost no CEAs → no LMIC-specific data
  - Prosthetics: Poor DW evidence → no LMIC CEAs → complete evidence void

The spectacles pathway shows what's possible: once you have credible DWs for
the "with" and "without" states, the CEA becomes straightforward and the
investment case becomes defensible. Wheelchairs are where spectacles were
20+ years ago — the intervention obviously works, but the numbers to prove
it to funders haven't been generated.

8. VALUE OF INFORMATION ANALYSIS
================================================================================

CURRENT STATE:
  - DW reduction from wheelchair provision: estimated 18-28%
  - This translates to a DW change of roughly 0.073-0.114 (from ~0.406 base)
  - Tornado sensitivity: ~$90/DALY swing on this single parameter
  - Pessimistic $/DALY is 7x base case, largely from DW uncertainty
  - The entire viability of wheelchair provision as a cost-effective
    intervention hinges on this parameter

WHAT A STUDY WOULD DO:
  - Narrow the DW reduction estimate from ±10pp to ±3-4pp
  - Reduce the $/DALY range from ~7x (base to pessimistic) to ~2x
  - Make the investment case either clearly compelling or clearly not
  - Inform ALL wheelchair provision programs globally, not just one

VALUE OF INFORMATION CALCULATION (ILLUSTRATIVE):
  - ATscale estimates ~100M people need wheelchairs globally
  - Even focusing on the ~2-5M wheelchairs distributed annually in LMICs
  - If better DW data shifts $50M-$500M in funding decisions over 10 years
    (either toward or away from wheelchair provision)
  - A $200K-$350K study has an expected VOI ratio of ~150:1 to 1,400:1
  - Even at the lowest estimate, this is among the highest-VOI research
    investments available in global health

WHAT IF THE STUDY SHOWS THE DW GAIN IS SMALL?
  - This is also extremely valuable information
  - If the real QALY gain is 0.05 instead of 0.19, wheelchair provision
    at $500/chair is ~$200/DALY — still potentially cost-effective
  - If the real QALY gain is 0.02, $/DALY exceeds $500 and resources
    should be redirected to spectacles or hearing aids
  - Knowing the answer either way beats guessing

9. RECOMMENDED MINIMUM VIABLE STUDY
================================================================================

DESIGN: Hybrid cross-sectional + paired comparison in 2 LMIC countries

COMPONENTS:
  A) EQ-5D-5L cross-sectional study
     - 200 wheelchair users (received chair 6-24 months ago) per country
     - 200 people awaiting wheelchair provision per country
     - Countries: Kenya + India (both have EQ-5D-5L value sets)
     - Piggybacked on existing wheelchair programs (Motivation, ICRC, etc.)
     - Primary endpoint: utility difference between groups
     - Secondary: WHODAS 2.0, participation measures

  B) Paired comparison community survey
     - 500 general population respondents per country
     - 4-6 wheelchair-specific vignettes + 20 GBD reference states
     - Face-to-face household interviews
     - GBD-compatible methodology (probit regression)
     - Primary endpoint: DW estimates for wheelchair-relevant states

BUDGET:
  Component A: $80K-$120K
  Component B: $70K-$120K
  Overhead/coordination: $30K-$50K
  TOTAL: $180K-$290K

TIMELINE: 14-18 months (3 months design, 6 months fieldwork, 5 months analysis)

OUTPUTS:
  1. First LMIC-specific QALY estimate for wheelchair provision
  2. First GBD-compatible DW for wheelchair-relevant mobility states in LMICs
  3. Publishable in Lancet Global Health or similar
  4. Directly usable in CEA models for all wheelchair funders globally
  5. Potential for adoption into GBD DW revisions

WHO SHOULD RUN IT:
  - PI: Hannah Kuper or David Boggs (LSHTM) — LMIC wheelchair expertise
  - Methodological advisor: Juanita Haagsma (Erasmus MC) — DW methods
  - Country leads: Established LSHTM/GDI Hub partners in Kenya and India
  - Wheelchair program partners: Motivation International, ICRC, or JAWS

10. SUMMARY OF KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. NO LMIC-SPECIFIC DW FOR WHEELCHAIR USERS EXISTS. Period.

2. The GBD DW system does not have a health state for "mobility-impaired
   person with/without a wheelchair." The closest proxies are "severe motor
   impairment" (0.402) and "treated SCI below neck" (0.296).

3. Only 2 full health economic analyses of wheelchair interventions exist
   globally, both for powered chairs in rich countries.

4. The evidence base is worse than for hearing aids and far worse than for
   spectacles, despite wheelchairs being a higher-burden intervention.

5. A credible DW study is feasible for $180K-$290K in 14-18 months.

6. The value of information ratio is extremely high (~150:1 to 1,400:1)
   given the magnitude of funding decisions this parameter influences.

7. The study design exists, the methodology is proven, the researchers are
   identifiable, and the cost is trivial relative to the decisions it would
   inform. The only thing missing is a funder.
